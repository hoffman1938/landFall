The house seed is +EV against an imbalanced crowd (the survivor payout is
convex in the struck pool), so the ring-fenced float earns a small, real
profit. Segregating it satisfies "the operator shall not profit"; releasing
the surplus to the jackpot is what satisfies "shall ultimately be lost".

--- A NOTE ON THE TWO BASES -----------------------------------------------
The published figure is on TOTAL handle; the measured one is on PLAYER handle.
The operator hold is levied on total handle while players supply only part of
it, so the player-basis figure sits below the published one by
hold x s/(1-s), where s is the seed share printed above — a few hundredths of
a point at a populated table. The seed itself is net-neutral to players over
the long run: it is ring-fenced, and its surplus is released to the jackpot.

The same arithmetic is why a player ALONE at a table sees a lower return: the
seed share rises as the table empties. That case is bounded and disclosed in
docs/02-math-verification.md section 6.2 rather than averaged away.
